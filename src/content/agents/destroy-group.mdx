# Destroy Group

This page states Destroy Group in the Developer Preview as trueseal-sync ADR-0029 decides it, with the API names of ADR-0028.

## Public wording

Use this wording when describing Destroy Group to users, and claim nothing beyond it.

> **Destroy Group ends the group for every device that receives it.** Each device that receives it passes the Destroy on to the members it knows about, deletes all group state, and starts over with a new identity. After that, no device that received it sends anything to any old device address, the stolen one included. To use TrueSeal together again, pair your devices from scratch.
>
> **What it does not do:**
> - It can't recover or erase anything the stolen device already received or stored.
> - It can't force the stolen device to wipe itself.
> - It doesn't reach a device that stays offline longer than the relay's message lifetime (<Fact id="relayTtlDefault" /> by default). That device keeps its old group until the user destroys or leaves it there too.
> - It doesn't complete while your device can't reach the relay. Your device shows "destroying" until it can.
> - Any current or former member can trigger it. That includes a stolen device, which can end your group, though that cuts it off from the group as well.

## Authority

- Any current member may call `destroyGroup()`, including a stolen device.
- A receiver honours a `Revoke` whose author appears in any Group Manifest it has held for this group, current or past.
- A `Revoke` from a device that never appeared in a held manifest is ignored.
- A hostile former member can therefore end the group. That affects availability only.
- Receivers keep the signing keys of every manifest they have held for the group until the group is wiped.

## Initiator

1. `destroyGroup()` persists `destroying` and, in the same transaction, queues one `Revoke` per member of the current view in the outbox.
2. It drops pending `Sync` sends and Pending Membership Changes without reporting them.
3. In `destroying` the device sends no `Sync`, runs no message handler and stays connected until the relay has accepted every `Revoke` push. There is no timeout.
4. It wipes the namespace's group state, outbox and dedup table, generates a fresh identity as the last step, and raises `statusChanged(notJoined, destroyed)`.

## Receiver

1. A valid `Revoke` moves a `member` or `leaving` device to `destroying`.
2. It queues a `Revoke` to every member of its own view except the author, by the same rules as the initiator.
3. It acks the `Revoke` to the relay after `destroying` commits (ADR-0026).
4. It waits and wipes as the initiator does.

Forwarding reaches members admitted concurrently and missing from the initiator's view. Each device forwards at most once, because a device in `destroying` or `notJoined` ignores further `Revoke`s. The worst case is about `maxGroupSize × (maxGroupSize − 1)` Revoke Blobs.

## States

| `status` | `destroyGroup()` | Valid incoming `Revoke` |
|---|---|---|
| `member` | → `destroying` | → `destroying`, forwards |
| `leaving` | → `destroying`, the leave is abandoned | → `destroying`, forwards |
| `pendingJoin` | Fails with `notMember{status}`{/* TODO: ADR-0029 says "typed error: not in a group"; notMember{status} is the ADR-0028 case that matches. */} | Ignored, no manifest to check the author against |
| `destroying` | No-op | Ignored |
| `notJoined` | Fails with `notMember{status}` | Ignored |

`destroying → notJoined` happens once the relay has accepted every queued `Revoke`.

## Crash points

| Point | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Before the `destroying` transaction commits | No change. On a receiver, the relay redelivers the unacked `Revoke`. |
| After the commit, during the pushes | `destroying` resumes on the next start. The outbox replays the `Revoke`s the relay hasn't accepted. |
| During the wipe | The wipe restarts. It never runs against a fresh identity, because the identity is replaced last. |

## After the wipe

- There is no key rotation inside the session. Every surviving device is `notJoined` with a fresh identity.
- The same `TrueSeal` object stays usable (ADR-0028). The user rebuilds the group by pairing again.
- The stolen device can send a join request like any other device. It is admitted only if a member calls `accept(request)` for it.
- Replaying a `Revoke` for a group that is already destroyed changes nothing, because every device has a fresh identity (ADR-0034).

## Choosing Destroy Group over removal

`remove(memberId)` relies on the removed device cooperating. Use `destroyGroup()` for a lost, stolen, compromised or hostile device. See [Membership](/agents/membership).

## What stays the app's job

- Showing `destroying` while it lasts, and never reporting the group as gone before `statusChanged(notJoined, destroyed)`.
- Deleting the app's own data after `statusChanged(notJoined, destroyed)`, if it should.
- Telling the user to destroy or leave the group on any device that was offline past the relay TTL.
- Guiding the user through pairing again.
