# Delivery Guarantees

trueseal-sync provides at-least-once transport within the relay TTL. It retries until the relay accepts a blob, and the relay retains that blob until the recipient process acknowledges its bytes.

That is not the same as exactly-once application processing. This page defines the boundary precisely.

## The two-sided mechanism

Durable transport comes from two independent mechanisms working in tandem:

**Sender side: the Outbox.** Every blob is written to the local Operation Log before it's pushed to the relay. It's marked undelivered until the relay confirms receipt. If the sender drops offline before the push succeeds, the blob stays in the Outbox. On reconnect, the session replays every undelivered entry to the relay in global sequence order. The sender never loses a blob — it stays locally until delivery is confirmed.

**Relay side: deferred delivery.** When the relay receives a blob addressed to an offline recipient, it stores it in that recipient's Inbox. When the recipient reconnects, the relay sends the Inbox and deletes each blob after the client acknowledges receipt of its bytes.

Combined: the sender keeps trying until the relay confirms durable storage. The relay holds the blob until the recipient process receives it.

## Application processing and Message IDs

The client sends `DeliverAck` immediately after receiving a frame, before decryption and before invoking application code. This keeps acknowledgement behavior independent of whether ciphertext is valid and prevents a malicious relay from using acknowledgements as a decryption oracle.

Consequently, a process crash after `DeliverAck` but before the application stores the payload can lose that application update. TrueSeal cannot atomically commit into an application's database.

Relay retries can also deliver the same Envelope more than once. Every SDK therefore exposes an opaque `messageId` that remains identical across re-delivery. Applications should persist `messageId` in the same transaction as their state change and ignore an ID already stored. This gives idempotent application behavior without making TrueSeal understand the payload.

## What "eventually" actually means

"Eventually" has one boundary, and it's the relay's TTL. Blobs held in the relay's Inbox past the TTL get reaped, delivered or not. A device that's been offline longer than the TTL will miss those blobs — they won't be on the relay when it comes back.

The sender's Outbox isn't subject to the relay TTL. An undelivered `Sync` entry stays in the Outbox for up to <Fact id="outboxExpiry" /> after `send()`, then is removed with `sendFailed(messageId, expired)`; control entries never expire. If a sender has been offline for weeks and reconnects, every entry still in the Outbox is replayed. But the relay will only accept and deliver those blobs to recipients who are online now or who reconnect within their own TTL window.

If a recipient remains offline past TTL, the relay can reap the blob. Even within TTL, TrueSeal guarantees transport to the recipient process, not a durable application commit.

## Order of delivery

Blobs arrive at `on_message` in arrival order — whatever order the relay hands them to the recipient's active Receive Session. That can be different from send order if the sender was offline and pushed a batch of Outbox entries on reconnect.

Causal ordering survives through parent hashes. Each Envelope points at the hash of its predecessor, so a recipient can tell whether a blob is causally downstream of one they haven't seen yet, regardless of the arrival order.

trueseal-sync doesn't buffer or reorder Envelopes on the recipient side. What arrives is what your callback gets. If you need strict causal ordering, use the parent hashes.

## Fan-out delivery

When a device calls `send()`, trueseal-sync builds one Envelope per current group member and pushes all of them over a single Push Session. Each Envelope is independently addressed and encrypted. The relay routes each one to the right Inbox.

The relay stores one independently addressed Envelope per recipient. In v0, the sender's Outbox entry is marked delivered when the relay confirms that the fan-out push was accepted. The sender does not receive per-recipient application confirmation.

## What delivery doesn't cover

**Newly added members.** A device that joins the group after a blob was sent will not receive that blob. The relay doesn't go back and retroactively route old blobs to new recipients. If you want new members to receive history, send it explicitly after `onMemberJoined` fires.

**Removed members.** A device removed from the group won't receive blobs sent after the manifest update that excludes it. It may still receive blobs that were in-flight at the time of removal — already addressed to its key and sitting in the relay's Inbox.

**Relay TTL expiry.** Blobs held past the relay TTL are reaped.

**Application crash window.** The client acknowledges received bytes before invoking application code. A crash between those events is outside the durable transport guarantee.

**Exactly-once processing.** Networks and separate databases cannot provide this automatically. Applications use `messageId` to make their own state changes idempotent.
