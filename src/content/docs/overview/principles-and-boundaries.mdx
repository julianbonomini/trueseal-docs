# Principles & Boundaries

trueseal is opinionated. Every choice in the stack traces back to a short list of things we won't compromise on, and a parallel list of things we deliberately won't do. Skim both and you'll know pretty quickly whether trueseal fits what you're building.

## TL;DR

| Principle | What it means |
|---|---|
| **End-to-End Encryption** | The relay can't read or forge your messages. It does see some metadata. |
| **No Accounts** | No accounts and no human identity. Stored messages carry no sender identity. |
| **Fault Tolerance** | Devices drop offline. Networks die. Any device can be gone for a long time and come back current. |
| **Durable Transport** | Sender and relay retain blobs across disconnects within documented boundaries. |
| **Revocability** | No device gets permanent access. The mechanism matches the threat. |
| **No Lock-In** | Any relay instance works. Self-host, public, switch around. Who runs it doesn't change what it can read. |

| Boundary | What we don't do |
|---|---|
| **Not a P2P System** | The relay is always in the path. |
| **Not a Real-Time Channel** | Background sync with strong delivery, not streaming. Sync might be fast, but "fast" isn't a guarantee we make. |
| **Not Exactly-Once Processing** | SDKs provide stable Message IDs; applications make their own writes idempotent. |
| **Not a Conflict Resolver** | Payload meaning and conflicts are yours. |
| **Not an Identity System** | A device is a keypair. Linking it to a human happens above this layer. |
| **Not a Durable Log** | The relay is a delivery buffer. Blobs are deleted on delivery. |
| **Not a Platform** | No admins, no hierarchies. If you're in the group, you're in. |

## Principles

### End-to-End Encryption

The relay can't read or forge your messages.

Everything is encrypted on the device before it goes anywhere. The relay receives ciphertext addressed to a recipient's public key. It holds no keys and can't read what it routes. It does see some metadata, such as who receives a message, its size and its time. [Here is exactly what it sees](/docs/overview/architecture#what-the-relay-actually-sees).

This is the only mode there is. There's no setting that loosens it.

### No Accounts

trueseal has no concept of a human identity. No accounts, no registration, nothing to sign up for.

A device is a long-term X25519 keypair, generated locally the first time the SDK runs (or generated by you, if you want to drive it yourself). The public key *is* the device's identity inside the protocol. Whoever owns the device is information that never enters the wire.

Every push to the relay happens over a short-lived connection opened with a fresh ephemeral keypair, so stored messages carry no sender identity. The relay can still often tell which device pushed, by IP address or timing, and which devices likely share a group, from how one send fans out to every member.

### Fault Tolerance

The primitive has to survive the environment it runs in, and the environment is messy.

Phones go in pockets. Wi-Fi drops in tunnels. Laptops sleep for a week. Any device can disappear for an indefinite stretch and come back fully current — that's the bar.

Undelivered blobs sit in the relay until the recipient reconnects, and they also sit in the sender's local outbox until the relay confirms delivery. Two safety nets, one on each side.

### Durable Transport

A blob survives sender disconnects until the relay accepts it. The relay then holds it for an offline recipient until receipt or TTL expiry.

Relay delivery is at least once, so duplicates are possible. Each SDK provides a stable Message ID for application deduplication. A client acknowledges frame receipt before application processing; exactly-once application commits are outside the transport boundary.

### Revocability

No device gets permanent access. Any current member can remove any other member at any time.

When the threat is mundane — selling a phone, leaving a project — removal is cooperative: the device is dropped from the Group Manifest and its future blobs get filtered out. When the threat is real, such as a stolen or compromised device, Destroy Group ends the group for every device that receives it. Each one deletes all group state and starts over with a new identity, and you pair your devices again. It can't erase what the stolen device already holds. See [Revocation](/docs/integrate/revocation).

The mechanism has to match the threat. Removal is fast and cheap. Destroy Group is the last resort. Both belong in the toolbox.

### No Lock-In

Any deployed relay works. Run your own, use someone else's, switch tomorrow. Who operates the relay doesn't change what it can read. It does decide whether your messages arrive.

There's no hosted-only feature, no proprietary extension, no operator (including us) you're forced to trust.

## Boundaries

The things trueseal won't do, on purpose.

### Not a P2P System

Devices don't talk to each other directly. The relay is always in the middle.

Real peer-to-peer over the open internet means NAT traversal, which means STUN and TURN to punch through home routers, office firewalls and mobile carriers — and when that fails (it fails a lot), TURN is just a relay anyway, wearing a different hat.

The trueseal relay solves the connectivity problem cleanly: outbound connections from both sides, no NAT gymnastics. The relay can't read what it carries, but it does see metadata, so the convenience has a privacy cost. [Here is exactly what it sees](/docs/overview/architecture#what-the-relay-actually-sees).

### Not a Real-Time Channel

No WebSockets, no sub-second delivery promises.

This is background sync with durable, retrying transport. The design optimises for correctness and privacy. Latency is whatever it happens to be.

### Not a Conflict Resolver

We transport every accepted blob toward each addressed device and expose stable Message IDs for duplicates. What happens when two offline devices write contradictory things is your call.

trueseal doesn't pick a winner and it doesn't merge. It has no opinion on your data model.

### Not an Identity System

No accounts. A device is a keypair, full stop. Display names, multi-device accounts, social graphs — all of that lives above this layer.

### Not a Durable Log

The relay is a buffer, not storage. Blobs are deleted on delivery.

If your app needs the full history of everything ever sent, keep that locally as messages arrive. The relay is not the archive.

### Not a Platform

No admin roles, no permission hierarchies, no hosted-only knobs. Any current member can do anything any other member can do.

If you need an admin who can kick people but can't be kicked themselves, build that above trueseal. It doesn't belong in the primitive.
